## The one rule that makes this skill work

**This skill does not carry brand rules. The Standard does.**

Read **The Standard**, table `tbleatoO3KDm2VZbS` in base `appP8PQe3dvqxosjR`, before
producing anything. It holds the living standard: brand grammar, the visual
standard, the learning loop, and what not to repeat. Niki edits it directly, so it
is always newer than this file.

This split exists because it has now failed twice.

On 2026-08-16 a predecessor skill carried the rule "CFBL Institute is scholarly,
use plates and ruled tables." The standard had already retired that layout and
recorded that photography-led content outperformed it. The skill was followed, the
work was wrong, and Niki spent a Sunday re-teaching something she had written down.

On 2026-09-10 the same failure repeated in a different form. The standard lived on
a Notion page, Notion was deleted, and the whole learning loop went with it. A
session then wrote the retired MUSA type-only layout into a shot note as if it
were current, because the only surviving copy of the rule was buried in a
reference file nobody had opened.

**Anything that changes belongs in The Standard. This file holds only what does
not.** If this file and The Standard disagree, The Standard wins. Then fix this
file.

## Standard of work

Present the strongest finished recommendation, not a list of possibilities. Do not
agree with a first idea automatically. When something is weak, repetitive,
off-brand, or unlikely to serve the objective, say so, explain why, and offer a
stronger version that keeps whatever was good. Sometimes the best recommendation
is to simplify, reuse, delay, or stop.

Narrate reasoning on consequential decisions. Skip it on routine work.

Niki supplies: her face and voice, her essays, her clinical judgment, genuinely
missing facts, and the final yes. Everything else this system carries. Do not push
production decisions back to her. Do not ask questions The Standard already
answers.

## The run, in order

**Retired 2026-09-10.** The Sunday-through-Saturday week planned Thursday to
Saturday of the week before was set on 2026-08-16 and Niki has killed it. Do not
reinstate it.

Plan a week ahead of whatever week is being produced, and get the plan in front of
her before that week opens. The cadence anchor is the blog, which runs **every
other week**, not the calendar week.

A blog release drags its own dates with it: post and email on day 0, social on day
2. Place those first, then fit everything else around them.

### 1. Read the standard and what already shipped

The Standard first, filtered to **Do not repeat** and **What performed**. Then the
Content Pipeline rows from the prior week whose Status is not Published. Those
move forward or get killed.

**Then read Shopify Files.** This is not optional and it is where the real history
lives. Every published asset is there with descriptive alt text that reads as
design specification. It is the only surviving record of which series exist, which
formats are established, and how far each numbered series has run. On 2026-09-10 a
session announced the launch of a series that had launched three weeks earlier,
because it built before it looked.

Content produced without these steps repeats rejected work, which is the fastest
way to lose trust in the system.

### 2. Verify what is actually live

Read the **Shopify product and page records directly**. Not a summarized web
fetch.

This is not a style preference. Three false claims once reached Niki in one cycle
because a page-summarizing fetch was trusted over the source: a workshop reported
sold out when seven seats were open, a group reported oversold when its inventory
was simply untracked, and a clinician's spotlight written with scope-of-practice
language that appeared nowhere in her bio. Read `product.descriptionHtml`,
`page.body`, `variant.inventoryQuantity`, `inventoryPolicy` and
`inventoryItem.tracked`. Untracked inventory is not zero inventory. DRAFT status
means it is not publicly purchasable.

Nothing gets promoted that is not verifiable and current. No invented workshops,
no revived expired events, no guessed dates or pricing, no assumed CE approval, no
manufactured urgency. Where the website contradicts an older document, including
this skill, the website wins and the discrepancy gets written into The Standard.

### 3. Allocate the week

> Four internal systems across two public identities, at differentiated intensity.
> "Three brands" here is legacy phrasing; see `CURRENT_SYSTEM.md`. Base footprints, expansion
triggers, and campaign structure are in `references/allocation.md`.

If the site check and carried-forward work do not fill the allocation, draw from
unscheduled Content Pipeline rows rather than inventing filler. If a brand
genuinely has nothing to say, produce nothing for it and record that. Silence
beats a post that exists to hit a number.

### 4. Plan each piece

One Content Pipeline row per piece. One job each, never stacked.

Audit the plan against the standing rules before production:

- **Founder presence** at least once, on camera, in voice, or genuine first
  person. Only Niki can supply it, so plan the week around securing it rather than
  hoping for it. Ask a full week ahead.
- **At least half conversational CTAs.** Comments, replies, tags and shares earn
  distribution. Registration links convert an audience that already exists.
- **Roughly three parts value to one part promotion** across the cycle. Count it,
  do not estimate it.
- **One lighthearted post**, never landing the same day as a heavy clinical piece.
- **Layout varies** from the prior cycle within each brand.
- **One brand per day on the shared handle.** Institute and MUSA both post as Dr.
  Niki Serravalle. Two on the same day compete with each other.

### 5. Produce

Photography first. Search Drive, Canva, Shopify Files and the website before
generating anything. Routing, the photographic standard, and the asset library are
in `references/production.md` and `references/asset-library.md`.

Every asset is photography-led. All three brands. No exceptions.

### 6. Quality gate

Every asset, before Niki sees it. Full checklist in `references/production.md`. If
an asset fails, rebuild it. Do not hand Niki weak design with a note explaining
the weakness.

### 7. The approval gate

Nothing is finished until Niki has seen it and said yes. Hard gate, every asset,
every caption, no exceptions.

Present as **"Here is what I made. Review it."** Lead with assets, not process.
Present the full week at once so she reviews in one sitting. For each asset show
the visual, the caption in full, the post date, the brand and account, and its
job. Never summarize a caption or describe an image she cannot see. A short cycle
summary goes at the end, never the top.

Then wait. Do not schedule, do not mark complete, and do not treat silence, a
general positive remark, or approval of one piece as approval of the week.

**Her response.** Approved pieces move Status to Approved. Revisions get her words
**verbatim** into Validation Notes, then the piece is rebuilt from the concept and
returned. If the same note appears twice on one piece, the concept is wrong, not
the execution. Killed pieces go to Blocked and the reason goes into The Standard
as a durable principle, not the adjective she used. "Too Canva" is not knowledge.
"A solid color ground with a boxed headshot reads as an academic slide" is.

Anything still awaiting approval when the week begins does not go out.

**Note the unresolved conflict.** The Airtable build carries a Publish Guard whose
design deliberately removes approval fields, on the reasoning that the previous
system died of four competing approval surfaces. That is in tension with this
gate. Until Niki settles it, the gate holds and the guard is treated as a
readiness check rather than an approval.

### 8. Close the cycle

Update every Content Pipeline row with its asset and final status.

Deliver approved assets in the form she actually posts from: the image files and
captions ready to copy, plus any email copy ready to paste.

**Move used source files into Drive / Marketing / Used.** A photograph or clip
that has run once is repurposing material, not fresh stock.

**Write next week's asks somewhere dated and owned, not into chat.** A list
delivered in a message disappears the moment she scrolls past it. Ask for
photographs weekly, a batch of ten to fifteen, and send the shot list rather than
asking her to plan it. Anything needing her voice must appear a full week ahead.
Never ask for a voiceover the day it runs.

### 9. The retro, one week later

At the start of the next run, close the prior cycle. Write what performed and what
not to repeat into The Standard as durable principles, so the standard itself
improves rather than just the log.

## Distribution

Treat nothing as published until it is. Never imply anything went out. The Buffer
publisher exists but has never run live, and posting currently stays with Niki.

## Non-negotiables

No em dashes, anywhere, ever. Regular hyphens only.

Verify against the Shopify record, never a summarized fetch.

Nothing promoted that is not currently live and verifiable.

Never flatten approved and pending. Getting CE status wrong is an ethics problem,
not a marketing one.

No generated clinicians, therapy offices, sessions, or clients.

Never put protected health information or identifiable client information into any
connected system.

Never read personnel files to find marketing material. Staff HR folders hold
credentialing, evaluations, contracts and departure records. Headshots come from
Drive / Marketing / Headshot.

No asset presented that has not passed the quality gate.

No asset scheduled, delivered as final, or treated as finished without Niki's
explicit approval on that specific piece. Silence is not approval.

No week planned without reading The Standard and Shopify Files.

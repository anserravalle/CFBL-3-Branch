# Allocation, brands, and campaigns

## Public architecture

Two accounts, not three.

**Center for Balanced Living** posts as the practice. **Dr. Niki Serravalle** is the public identity for professional education, books, essays, and Make It Make Sense. CFBL Institute and MUSA are imprints and divisions under her name, not standalone social accounts unless directed.

Brand governs voice and visual grammar. Account governs where it posts. Those are different questions and the Content row answers both.

## Base footprint, a normal week

Equal volume across three brands is how all three end up thin, because the practice generates far more genuine weekly news than a training division or an author imprint does. Differentiated intensity: a floor so nothing goes dark, automatic expansion for whichever brand has something real happening.

**Center for Balanced Living**, four to five pieces. Groups, availability, staff, assessment and therapy education, community, blogs, the property, referral material. One of these is the week's lighthearted post.

**CFBL Institute**, one to two pieces. Clinician-facing thought leadership. Clinical reasoning, diagnostic thinking, EMDR and Nature-Informed EMDR, treatment planning, what clinicians commonly misunderstand. This floor holds even with nothing to sell, because clinician trust is built continuously and cannot be manufactured during a launch week.

**MUSA**, one piece. An essay fragment, a line from the work in progress, a note on writing, a passage on memory or identity. Smallest footprint, most restrained thing in the feed.

**Make It Make Sense** runs when the week has a genuine misunderstanding worth correcting. Not forced to cadence.

Founder presence can be satisfied by any brand and often lands best on Institute or MUSA, where Niki is the identity.

## Expansion triggers

The affected brand takes a larger share, drawing down from the practice rather than adding volume on top.

**Institute expands** for an open cohort, a registration deadline inside four weeks, a live training, a CE-approved course, a conference or speaking date, or a new professional offering. Expanded weeks usually add a Gamma deliverable.

**MUSA expands** for a publication date, a released essay, a book announcement, a pre-order window, a reading, or a literary speaking engagement.

**The practice expands** when a group is filling, a workshop is open, a clinician has joined, availability has opened, or there is a community event or local recognition.

Two triggers in one week stagger across days rather than competing. Ratios hold across the full cycle, not per brand.

## Guardrails

Three to one value over promotion is measured across the whole cycle. A launch week does not license stacking offers.

The conversational CTA requirement applies across the cycle. Launch weeks are exactly when it gets dropped, and dropping it is what turns an audience into a list that stops engaging.

Layout rotation is enforced per brand against the prior cycle. A launch is not an excuse to repeat the format that worked last time.

If a brand has nothing to say, produce nothing for it. Record it and pull from an unscheduled Content Pipeline row next week.

## Campaigns

Campaigns run through the same weekly loop at higher intensity. Do not build a parallel process.

A campaign is a sequence across three to six cycles anchored to a real date. Work backward from the deadline.

**Early, four to six weeks out.** Establish the problem the offer solves. Value-forward, conversational, almost no selling. This is where trust is built and it is the part most often skipped.

**Middle, two to four weeks out.** Introduce the offer and its specifics. Who it is for, what it covers, what it does not. Gamma deliverables usually land here.

**Late, inside two weeks.** Registration, logistics, deadlines, social proof. Transactional CTAs concentrate here, and this is the only stretch where the ratio tilts.

**Close.** Deadline, then a genuine thank-you or recap that returns to value.

Social proof accumulates across the campaign. Collect consensus signals, participant counts, group photos, and permissioned voices continuously and work them in as they arrive. Absent social proof is a conversion gap, not a cosmetic one.

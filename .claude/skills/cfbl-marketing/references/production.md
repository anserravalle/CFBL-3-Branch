# Production

## The photographic standard

**Every social asset is photography-led. All three brands. No exceptions.**

A real editorial image is the field, with type set over or within it. Type on a solid color ground is retired. So are the ruled-table Institute card and the image-sparse MUSA page. This is not only taste. As of the week of 2026-08-11, photography-led posts measurably outperformed type-led conference-slide content.

The three print traditions below set **tone, not layout**. They describe how each brand's photography and type feel, never permission to set flat type on a color.

**Center for Balanced Living is editorial.** A well-made magazine feature. Photography carries the weight, type sits confidently over and beside it. Warm, human, credible. Asymmetric, generous whitespace with a purpose.

**CFBL Institute is elevated and scholarly in tone, still photography-led.** Natural light, plants, rooms, books, objects, a clinician's desk. Restrained type over the image. Serif headline, forest and cream and sage. No ruled tables, no figure plates, no handout cards. On a piece whose job is registration, the structure is photograph, badge, headline, one scannable detail line, one solid CTA bar.

**MUSA is literary in tone, still photography-led.** Moody atmosphere, books, paper, letters, shadow, deep contrast. A single line set into the image. Image-oblique, never image-absent. Oxblood, bone, parchment, walnut, ink.

## Reject and rebuild

If it looks like an academic slideshow, a solid rectangle with a cropped headshot in a box beside a paragraph, a half-photo half-text-column split, a standard Canva post, a therapist infographic, a CE handout, a corporate template, a motivational graphic, a university slide, a wellness account, or an AI-generated social post. Correct palette and type do not save a flat layout.

Avoid icon rows, boxed clutter, full slides of text, bullet-heavy graphics, generic AI therapy imagery, stock smiling groups, spa flat-lays of stones and candles and eucalyptus, illustrated path or journey imagery, and people pretending to be clients.

## Photography priority

Search real sources before generating. Drive / Marketing, then Canva, then the Shopify file library, then the website. Generate only when the required image genuinely does not exist.

Generated imagery is for atmosphere, objects, conceptual scenes, close detail, nature, landscape, paper, books, rooms and shadow. **Never for clinicians, therapy offices, sessions, clients, or testimonials.**

Reuse real people and places so the audience builds visual recognition. Do not run two posts on the same photograph in one week without spacing them.

Register guide: warm and soft portraits carry the practice and relatable content. Moody and elevated portraits carry Institute and MUSA. Bold or edgy portraits are for myth-busting only and stay off the clinical practice page, where warmth builds more trust than edge.

Route by job, not by habit. A polished output from the wrong tool is still wrong.

**Gamma** is not a weekly social tool. Its honest fit is Institute documents and MUSA long form. Set the theme deliberately and pass `imageOptions.source` as `noImages` or `placeholder`, then drop in real photography rather than letting it generate wellness imagery.

**Authored HTML** rendered headless at 1080x1350 or 1080x1920 is the most reliable route to exact brand typography and layout control. Canva templates flatten the distinctions between the three brands; that is the failure mode to avoid.

## Formats

**Carousels** read as editorial spreads, not worksheets. One idea per frame, photography throughout, a large headline, a short supporting line. Complexity belongs in the caption.

**Reels** are built from deliberate visual moments, not continuous generated video. Script, then four to seven moments, then 9:16 photographic frames graded to match, then assembly with simple transitions. Each frame should hold as a still. Preferred motion: slow zoom, subtle pan, dissolve, gentle parallax, text reveal. Avoid frantic motion and trendy transition packs.

**The format deck** rotates so the feed reads designed rather than stamped out: full-bleed cover, type-led field, bold split, duotone, diptych, wide-margin single line. Keep the signature constant, rotate the format.

**The humor pillar** runs weekly. Warm first, funny second. Clever, not silly. Never at a client's expense, never diagnosing for laughs, always in brand typography. Flagship format is **Permission Slips**, a mock slip granting rest, boundaries, or grace, signed by Niki. It still needs a photographic ground; the slip works as a physical object lying on a real surface.

## Captions

Niki's voice: clear, psychologically sophisticated, direct, research-informed, human, clinically responsible, useful, never salesy. Lead with recognition so the reader feels seen before they feel sold to.

Avoid manufactured urgency, empty inspiration, diagnosis-by-caption, oversimplification, guaranteed outcomes, wellness cliches, generic AI phrasing, and excessive emojis. Hashtags targeted and credible, not spammy. Local CFBL hashtags for practice content.

Name things correctly. The blog is **Make It Make Sense**, not "the blog."

**No em dashes. Regular hyphens only.**

## Quality gate

Before Niki sees anything:

Does it belong to a brand by its structure, not only its palette? Would a viewer know the brand without the handle? Is photography carrying the composition? Is the offer verified against the Shopify record? Is the CTA accurate to what is live? Is the clinical language responsible? Does the layout differ from the prior cycle? Is there one clear idea? Is the copy restrained? Is the headline strong? Does it read as a publication or campaign rather than a social template? Is every factual claim traceable to a source record rather than a summary?

Any no means rebuild before presenting.

This month: ten minutes of morning light

Now that the clocks have fallen back, the evenings go dark early, and many people notice their sleep and mood shift with them. One of the simplest things to try is light early in the day. Ten to twenty minutes outdoors in the first hour after waking gives the body's internal clock a clear signal about when the day begins. Even on an overcast morning, outdoor light is many times brighter than a lit room, so the window is no substitute for the porch.

Attach it to something you already do: coffee outside, or the long way to the car.

This is general education, not clinical care. If you have bipolar disorder, talk with your prescriber before adding light exposure. If changes in sleep or mood last, or get in the way of your life, talk with your doctor or a licensed clinician.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988.

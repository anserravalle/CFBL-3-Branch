We Should Not Need a Courtroom

I had postpartum depression three times.

The first time, I did not really know what it was. I had just had a baby and everything had changed, and I assumed the disconnection and the flat mood were part of that.

The second time, I recognized it as depression. I did not feel connected to her. I was going through the motions with her.

The third time, it lasted longer.

Women are told about gestational diabetes. They are warned about hemorrhage. Most are never told any of this.

This summer, a trial about a mother and postpartum psychosis became something the whole country watched. I will not offer an opinion on the woman at the center of it. This essay is about what the case brought up: what we did not know, how quickly we decided anyway, why we judge mothers differently, and how little the research has moved.

Part One, Three times, and Part Two, What we should have known first, are up now. Part Three comes Monday.
